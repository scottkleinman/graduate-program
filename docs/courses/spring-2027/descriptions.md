Below are course descriptions submitted by instructors for Spring 2027 graduate courses and certain 400-level English classes likely to be of interest to grad students. More descriptions will be added as they are received by the Graduate Adviser.

### ENGL 507: Introduction to Graduate Studies -- Kleinman

English 507 introduces students to graduate-level research and writing skills across the three options in the English MA program: Literature, Creative Writing, and Rhetoric and Composition.

### ENGL 525CBS: Comic Book Superheroes -- Hatfield

Intensive study of the superhero genre in US comic books from the mid-1930s onward, with attention to its enduring controversies. We will survey the genre’s history during the first third of the course, then concentrate on recent examples of academic superhero criticism and theory. Expect discussion of form, aesthetics, seriality, politics, gender, sexuality, race, disability, and fandom. Scholars studied may include, for example, Umberto Eco, Ramzi Fawaz, Samantha Langsdale, Anna Peppard, Darieck Scott, and Daniel Stein. Students will share reading responses during most weeks, take turns leading discussion, brainstorm a superhero of their own, and write a final seminar paper engaging with the critical literature.

### ENGL 525SHS: Shakespeare Sonnet -- Bryson

Though Shakespeare is often the first name that comes to mind when thinking of the Sonnet, he is actually a late practitioner of an already-centuries old form. This course will trace the sonnet's history, starting with its Persian and Arabic roots in the ghazal tradition, working through the canso tradition of the Provencal troubadours, and into the dolce stil novo tradition of the Sicilian and Italian poets of the 13th-15th centuries, before arriving in England with Shakespeare's predecessors and Shakespeare himself.

### ENGL 622: The Poetics of Intimacy: Love and Eroticism in Poetry -- Hall

Tina Turner famously questioned *What's love got to do with it?* This graduate seminar examines the erotic and erotic love as a central problem and generative force in the history of poetry. Focusing on poetry across periods, cultures, and literary traditions, this course considers how poets represent desire, intimacy, embodiment, longing, pleasure, absence, and loss. Readings will engage theories of eros, sexuality, gender, embodiment, and subjectivity alongside primary poetic texts. As scholars and poets, we will give particular attention to the ways language and form mediate erotic experience as well as to the tensions between private desire and cultural, ethical, and political structures. We will not only ask Turner's famous question, but we'll ask How do we write love? How do we write the erotic? How do we articulate the body? Navigate the bawdy? Code language? Primary texts will range from ancient to contemporary, including Sappho, Ovid, Shakespeare, Whitman, Dickinson, Neruda, Lorde, Olds, Chen, Hickock, and Vuong, among others. Secondary readings may include Bataille, Foucault, Sedgwick, Lorde, and Carson.

Students may take this course for literature credit or poetry workshop credit, electing either to write a brief collection of erotic/love poems with a critical preface OR a scholarly examination of erotic/love poetry through the study of selected primary texts for a conference-primed paper.

### ENGL 623: Novel/Style/Theory -- Byler

This course will focus on the works of three nineteenth-century novelists known for their distinctive prose styles: Jane Austen, Charles Dickens, and Henry James. Alongside works including *Emma* (1815), *A Christmas Carol* (1843), and *What Maisie Knew* (1897), we wil investigate theories of novelistic style as well as theories of the novel and theories of reading. Students will also have the opportunity to produce critical and creative work related to CSUN’s Harry Stone Collection of harles Dickens materials, which will be the focus of a year-long exhibition in 2028-2029. For more information, contact Professor Byler at Lauren.Byler@csun.edu.

### ENGL 698D: Graduate Project -- Stallcup

This course is one of the available choices for the culminating experience in the English department M.A. program. In significantly revising existing written work, students will practice the tools of research used in the field of English Studies. Students in the Literature and the Rhetoric and Composition Options will focus on revising and developing a previously written essay. Additionally, students will gain experience with professional conferences, presentations and productions.

### DH 480: Digital Humanities Studies and Projects -- Kleinman

This will be a hands-on research course where you will work on two major Digital Humanities projects: The [New Variorum Shakespeare Project](https://newvariorumshakespeare.org/){target="_blank"} and the Lexos a text analysis tool. The New Variorum Shakespeare project began in the nineteenth century as an attempt to document every prior edition of Shakespeare’s plays while producing new editions. Since 2019, the project has expanded into digital publication (led by a graduate of our MA program here at CSUN). As part of the project, you will transcribe editions of Shakespeare's plays from a variety of periods and explore how they can be compared digitally using Lexos. You will make lasting contributions to one or both projects and will receive credit for your contributions on the project websites. The course is a chance to participate actual scholarly research as opposed to the traditional course assignments for a grade. You will gain valuable experience in digital humanities methodologies and collaborative research practices.

The course is scheduled on Tuesdays at 2:30. However, it will be structured in a hybrid format, combining in-person and online components to facilitate both hands-on work on campus and individual work and discussion outside of scheduled class times.

DH 480 is available as a substitute for the Language Requirement or the Seminar in Textual or Cultural Studies, as well as for a graduate Elective. In some circumstances, it may substitute for a Literature requirement (consult the Graduate Adviser).
